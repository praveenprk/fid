# Plan: real-world feeds shown live on fid's site, including space (v0.3.0)

Date: 2026-11-27. Status: approved by the governor first-hand on
2026-11-28, with Q1–Q5 answered by their recommendations (below). Q6
stays paused. Work follows "Order of work", one reviewed PR per step.
Asked by: the governor, through engineering-fa (2026-11-26).

## What exists today

- The site (`site/`) is built by `.github/workflows/site.yml` and deployed
  to GitHub Pages as a build artifact. Nothing is committed back to `main`.
  It rebuilds on pushes to `main` that touch `calibration/` or `site/`,
  and weekly. In that build, the CEO and METAR studies are re-run on fresh
  data.
- `site/shell.html` already has a live board. The browser fetches the
  latest METAR for 10 US airports from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet (IEM)
  every 5 minutes. It recomputes fid's fidelity every second from the
  report's age, using the half-life and baseline published in
  `data/findings.json`. Source class is instrument (0.97), and the floor
  comes from `examples/floor.yaml` (0.75).
- The fidelity formula in the page is a JavaScript copy of fid's decay. No
  test ties it to the library.
- Calibrated registry entries (`examples/half-lives.yaml`, shipped in the
  crate) that relate to live feeds: `airport_flight_category`,
  `airport_ifr`, `airport_temperature` (METAR) and `vessel_position`
  (NOAA AIS, ±1 km). There is nothing for space.

## Principle

The site shows a fid number only where a committed, held-out calibration
backs it. That covers what docs/FINDINGS.md and `examples/half-lives.yaml`
already say. A feed without such a study shows its raw age and the label
"not calibrated", never a fid number. A feed that cannot be reached shows
UNKNOWN, never its last value presented as live. So the site makes the
paper's results visible and adds no claim the paper does not make. The
exception is any study the governor chooses to add (Q2), which goes
through calibration/ and FINDINGS first, like every other study.

## Feeds

Feeds marked "to confirm" were checked for cross-origin (CORS) headers on
2026-11-26 (`curl -H Origin:`). Their terms must be re-read before the
feed is built.

### 1. Airport weather (METAR): live already, kept

- **Source and terms:** IEM `json/current.py` (Iowa State University).
  The data is free to use, and IEM asks to be credited. aviationweather.gov
  sends no CORS header, so the browser cannot fetch it directly.
- **Fetch:** in the browser, directly (`Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`).
- **fid:** `airport_flight_category`, `airport_ifr` and
  `airport_temperature`, as today.
- **Live:** the report is re-fetched every 5 minutes, and the score ticks
  every second.
- **Failure:** today a failed fetch keeps the old card. Change: a station
  that cannot be fetched shows UNKNOWN after one missed refresh.
- **Paper cost:** none. These are FINDINGS results.

### 2. Ships (AIS): no free live source

- **Source:** fid's study uses NOAA's AIS archive
  (coast.noaa.gov/htdata/CMSP/AISDataHandler). That archive is daily files
  published long after the fact. It is not a live feed. The free live AIS
  services found need an API key, which would be exposed in the browser,
  and their terms differ.
- **Proposal:** keep ships as a recorded result (Q5). Optionally, replay a
  committed day at real speed with a clear "replay of <date>" label, never
  presented as live.
- **Paper cost:** none.

### 3. Satellites: element-set (TLE) age and position fidelity (new)

- **Source and terms:** CelesTrak GP data (`gp.php`, JSON). It sends
  `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`. CelesTrak asks clients not to
  download the same data again within its update cycle of about 2 hours
  (usage policy, to confirm). The element sets come from the US Space
  Force (18 SDS) by way of Space-Track.org.
- **Fetch:** a scheduled job, not the browser (Q3). Every 2 hours the job
  fetches one group (the tracked objects, Q1) and writes `data/tle.json`
  into the Pages artifact. That is one download per cycle whatever the
  traffic, and nothing is committed to `main`.
- **Computation:** the browser propagates each element set to now with
  SGP4 (satellite.js, MIT, pinned with an SRI hash). It shows:
  - the element set's age (now minus epoch);
  - fid's fidelity that the propagated position is still within the
    tolerance (Q4) of the true position.

  That needs a new registry entry, `satellite_position_tle`, with states by
  orbit regime, because decay depends on drag: low LEO (< 500 km, ISS
  class), LEO, MEO (GNSS) and GEO. The entry comes from a new study,
  `calibration/satellites_tle.py`. For pairs of successive element sets of
  the same object, the study propagates the older set to the newer set's
  epoch and to later epochs. It compares the result with the newer set's
  own position, counts how often the error is within tolerance at each
  age, and fits half-life and baseline per regime with a held-out split by
  object, as the AIS study does. History source: Q6.
- **Live:** the position and fidelity tick every second, and the element
  sets refresh every 2 hours. The card is "live" only while `data/tle.json`
  is under 6 hours old. Beyond that, or if it is missing, the card shows
  UNKNOWN.
- **Paper cost:** a new study and a new FINDINGS entry. That adds claims,
  so it is the governor's choice (Q2). Until the study lands, the cards
  show the age only, as "not calibrated".

### 4. The ISS now

- The ISS is one of the satellites in feed 3 (NORAD 25544), shown first.
  Its position is the SGP4 propagation of its latest element set, so it
  needs no separate position API. open-notify is http-only, which an https
  page would block as mixed content. wheretheiss.at is rate-limited and
  adds nothing that propagation does not already give.
- **"How fast a position report goes stale":** two numbers, labelled
  differently.
  - (a) The fid fidelity of the element-set prediction, from the
    `satellite_position_tle` low-LEO state (calibrated, after Q2).
  - (b) A fixed latitude/longitude reported without propagation goes stale
    in seconds: at about 7.66 km/s it leaves a 1 km tolerance in about
    0.13 s. That is arithmetic, shown as an illustration, not as a fid
    calibration.
- **Paper cost:** (a) as feed 3; (b) none, because it is not a claim
  about data.

### 5. Space weather: NOAA SWPC Kp and solar wind (new)

- **Source and terms:** NOAA SWPC `services.swpc.noaa.gov`. It is a US
  Government work, in the public domain. Kp comes from
  `products/noaa-planetary-k-index.json`, and the real-time solar wind
  from `json/rtsw/rtsw_wind_1m.json`. Both send
  `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` and `cache-control: max-age=60`.
- **Fetch:** in the browser, directly. Kp every 15 minutes (Kp is
  3-hourly; the planetary estimate updates more often). Solar wind every
  minute.
- **fid:** two candidate facts, each needing its own study before it gets
  a number:
  - `kp_index`: "Kp is still within ±1 of the reported value". History
    from GFZ Potsdam's Kp series since 1932 (CC BY 4.0, credit required).
  - `solar_wind_speed`: "speed still within ±50 km/s". History from
    NASA OMNI (public).

  Both would be fitted like `airport_temperature`, by checking for a
  periodic echo; solar rotation is about 27 days.
- **Live:** the score ticks every second. A card is live while its last
  fetch is under twice its refresh interval old; after that it shows
  UNKNOWN.
- **Paper cost:** two new studies. This plan proposes age only for v0.3.0
  (Q2).

## Shared rules for every card

1. **Failure:** if a fetch fails or the data is older than its feed allows,
   the card shows UNKNOWN (fid's indeterminate state). It also shows when
   the feed was last reached. It never shows a number as live.
2. **Two kinds of age:** the age of the report (fid's subject) is not the
   age of the fetch (the site's health). A report that is old but freshly
   fetched gets a low fid score; that is the point. A failed fetch gets
   UNKNOWN, because a newer report may exist.
3. **The formula stays tied to fid:** a test in the `lint` job (node, no
   new dependency) runs the page's `fidelity()` against
   the `fidelity` and `time_to_floor` cases in conformance/vectors.json, so the site cannot drift from
   the library.
4. **Attribution:** every card names its source and links to its terms.
5. **Load on sources:** each visitor fetches at the intervals above and
   only while the tab is visible (`document.visibilityState`).

## Scheduled job (feed 3)

- A new `live` job in `site.yml`, on `schedule: cron "7 */2 * * *"`. It
  fetches CelesTrak once, writes `data/tle.json` with a fetched-at time,
  and redeploys Pages with the rest of the site unchanged. It uses the
  same permissions as today (contents read; pages and id-token write), and
  nothing is committed. Twelve deploys a day is within Pages' limits.
- If CelesTrak is down, the job keeps the previous artifact's
  `data/tle.json`, and the page's 6-hour rule shows UNKNOWN when it ages
  out.
- This workflow change is security-labelled (workflow permissions and
  triggers, ENG-STD-005 §2.5), so PK merges it.

## Questions for the governor (each with a recommendation)

**Answered (governor, first-hand, 2026-11-28): approved as recommended.**
Q1 A, Q2 A, Q3 A, Q4 C, Q5 A. Q6 is not answered (paused).

These are choices within the plan, not `Q-NNN` entries: approving the
plan answers them with the recommendation unless you say otherwise.
Raising them in docs/QUESTIONS.md would answer them automatically under
DECISION-010, which would defeat the approval.

- **Q1. Which satellites?**
  - A: ISS plus about 20 chosen objects (Hubble, GNSS, GEO weather
    satellites, a few Starlink, a rocket body).
  - B: a whole CelesTrak group (e.g. `stations`, `active`).
  - **Recommend A**, because it covers every orbit regime, stays readable,
    and is one small fetch.
- **Q2. New studies in v0.3.0?**
  - A: TLE only. Kp and solar wind show age only.
  - B: all three.
  - C: none, age only everywhere.
  - **Recommend A**, because TLE staleness has clean ground truth (the
    next element set) and is the textbook case. Each other study adds a
    claim to review.
- **Q3. TLE fetch: browser or scheduled job?**
  - A: a job every 2 hours into Pages.
  - B: the browser directly.
  - **Recommend A**, because it keeps to CelesTrak's once-per-cycle
    request whatever the traffic.
- **Q4. Position tolerance for "still right"?**
  - A: 1 km, matching `vessel_position`.
  - B: 10 km.
  - C: both, with the site showing 1 km.
  - **Recommend C**, because the study reports both and the page stays
    comparable with ships.
- **Q5. Ships?**
  - A: recorded results only.
  - B: add a labelled replay of a committed day.
  - C: find a keyed live feed.
  - **Recommend A** for v0.3.0, because a key in the browser is exposed,
    and a replay is easy to misread as live.
- **Q6. TLE history for the study?** *Paused by the governor
  (2026-11-27, relayed by engineering-fa): the Space-Track option is not
  pursued for now, and approving the plan does not answer Q6.*
  - A: Space-Track.org history, run locally with the governor's account,
    committing only fitted results, as the other studies do (terms on
    derived results to confirm).
  - B: archive CelesTrak forward from now and calibrate after enough
    weeks.
  - **Recommend A** (B as a fallback if the terms forbid it), because it
    gives years of pairs now, and no raw element sets are redistributed.
